# Cruise-phase operational and systems technologies for fuel-burn reduction (300-seat medium-range widebody, EIS 2040, 100% SAF) — status October 2026

Note on method: the airbus.com, aviationweek.com and frenchbee.com pages could not be fetched (DNS failure in this environment). Figures from those sources are taken from search snippets and from secondary outlets that reproduce Airbus press releases, and are labelled as such. Reference mission: about 6.5 h block, which is roughly 5.5–6 h of cruise.

## Q1: How much fuel per flight can each realistically save on a ~6.5 h mission, and which add on top of airframe/engine improvements?

### Takeaway
Measured cruise-phase operational savings are each in the single-digit percent range. Wake energy retrieval (WER) is the largest at about 5% for the follower aircraft only, so about 2.5% averaged across a pair, and it applies only to flights that can be paired. ATM/trajectory optimisation is worth about 1–6% of gate-to-gate fuel, depending on how inefficient the airspace is today. Aft-CG control, statistical or reduced contingency fuel and weight reduction are each worth roughly 0.5–1.5%. Contrail avoidance costs fuel (0 to +2% on the flights that deviate) rather than saving it. Almost all of these are additive with airframe and engine gains, because they act on drag, weight or trajectory rather than propulsive efficiency. The exception is that each percentage applies to a smaller fuel base once the aircraft itself is more efficient.

### Cited Findings

**Formation flight / wake energy retrieval (WER)**
- **fello'fly final demonstration, 9 November 2021.** Two Airbus A350 test aircraft flew Toulouse–Montreal in oceanic airspace: MSN1 as leader and MSN59 as follower, about 3 km apart. Airbus reported more than 6 t of CO2 saved and said this "confirmed the potential for up to 5% fuel savings on long-haul flights". — [Airdatanews](https://airdatanews.com/airbus-performs-a-formation-flight-with-two-a350s-in-order-to-save-fuel); [Aerospace Testing International](https://www.aerospacetestinginternational.com/news/airbus-completes-key-fellofly-wake-energy-retrieval-flight-tests.html)
- **Measured detail for that flight.** Skies Magazine reported that the pair spent about 80% of the flight in formation and the follower saved "over 5% (or around 2 tonnes)" of fuel. Other coverage gives a separation of about 1.2 nm (2.2 km). Separation figures therefore differ between sources (about 2.2 km against about 3 km). — [Skies Magazine](https://skiesmag.com/?p=335039); [AirInsight](https://airinsight.com/airbus-fellofly-validates-key-step-toward-5-fuel-cuts/)
- **Claim against measurement.** About 2 t of fuel is consistent with about 6 t of CO2, since 1 t of fuel gives about 3.16 t of CO2. Some secondary analyses quote 5–10% per trip. Airbus's own headline figure is "up to 5%". — [AirInsight](https://airinsight.com/airbus-fellofly-validates-key-step-toward-5-fuel-cuts/)
- **Who was on board in 2021.** Pilots from SAS and French bee flew as observers. The air navigation service providers (ANSPs) were DSNA, NATS, NAV CANADA, EUROCONTROL and IAA. The two A350s landed at Montréal-Trudeau at 10:34 and 10:37 local time after a flight of less than 8 h. — [French bee press release (PDF)](https://prod.frenchbee.com/sites/default/files/2021-12/211109_FelloFlyFlight_VF_EN.pdf) (snippet only; the page could not be fetched)
- **Earlier Airbus test.** Airbus flew an A350 behind an A380 and Aviation Week reported a 12% fuel saving. This was a flight-test value at close spacing, not an operational figure. — [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/special-topics/sustainability/airbus-boeing-working-next-steps-fuel-saving-wake-surfing) (snippet)
- **GEESE (SESAR 3 JU, EU co-funded, launched 2023, runs to about mid-2026).** Airbus led the project with Air France, Delta, French bee and Virgin Atlantic, using A350s. The ANSPs were AirNav Ireland, DSNA, EUROCONTROL and NATS. Eight North Atlantic trial flights took place in September–October 2025.
  - The trials validated the rendezvous process only: guiding two aircraft to meet at a set time and place while keeping full vertical separation and staying within ATC rules.
  - Wake energy retrieval itself has not yet been flown on commercial flights.
  - The next step stated by the partners is to get the authorities' support so that the operational concept can be certified.
  - Sources: [Airbus newsroom, Feb 2026](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026-02-geese-and-atm-wake-energy-retrieval-collaboration-achieves-new-milestones); [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/?p=98378); [Aerospace Testing International](https://www.aerospacetestinginternational.com/news/airbus-completes-key-fellofly-wake-energy-retrieval-flight-tests.html)
- **Earlier timeline missed.** Earlier coverage said Airbus hoped to put fello'fly into service "by the middle of the decade". That has not happened as of 2026. — [Aerospace Testing International](https://www.aerospacetestinginternational.com/news/airbus-completes-key-fellofly-wake-energy-retrieval-flight-tests.html)
- **Boeing ecoDemonstrator (2018).** Boeing flight-tested wake surfing with its 777F ecoDemonstrator flying behind a FedEx 777F. The ecoDemonstrator programme investigated wake predictions to allow extended-formation "wake surfing". No Boeing fuel figure was found. — [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/special-topics/sustainability/airbus-boeing-working-next-steps-fuel-saving-wake-surfing); [Boeing ecoDemonstrator history](https://www.boeing.com/innovation/sustainability/ecodemonstrator/history)
- **NASA Armstrong (2017).** NASA flew a C-20A as the trail aircraft in the wake of a G-III at about 4,000 ft trail distance. This was not a joint test with Boeing. — [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180004526/downloads/20180004526.pdf)
- **USAF/AFRL $AVE ("Surfing Aircraft Vortices for Energy", 2012–13, with DARPA, Boeing and NASA Dryden).** C-17s flew 4,000 ft or more behind the leader.
  - The 2012 tests gave early indications of up to about 10% lower fuel consumption on the trail aircraft.
  - The 2013 follow-on reported "double-digit" savings.
  - Sources: [Wright-Patterson AFB](https://www.wpafb.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/399354/experimental-surfing-formation-reduces-fuel-use-cost/); [USAF](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/110318/vortex-surfing-could-be-revolutionary/)

**Free-route airspace (FRA), optimised trajectories and 4D trajectory-based operations (TBO)**
- **SESAR 2020 ToBeFREE validation (optimised free routing in cross-border upper airspace).** 27 kg less fuel and 84 kg less CO2 per flight, about 772,000 t of CO2 a year. This holds only with an adequate FRA structure and the right ATC support tools. — [SESAR JU](https://sesarju.eu/node/689)
- **FRAMaK project (Belgium, Germany, Luxembourg, Netherlands; SESAR-sponsored).** About 1.5 million NM a year, roughly 9,000 t of fuel and 30,000 t of CO2 saved. — [SESAR JU](https://www.sesarju.eu/node/153)
- **SECSI FRA (Central to South-East Europe).** Up to 8,000 kg of fuel saved per day across the region, based on shortest-route assignment. — [ATC Network / DFS](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/dfs-deutsche-flugsicherung-gmbh/significant-savings-on-european-flight-routes)
- **EUROCONTROL estimate for free route.** Up to "2/3%" of total fuel burn, depending on current airspace inefficiency. The wording probably means 2–3%. — [EUROCONTROL via ICAO](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/EURNAT/Documents/EUR%20and%20Nat%20Docs/Environment/2019%20Regional%20Seminar%20on%20Environment/EUROCONTROL-Operational-mitigations_final.pdf)
- **European gate-to-gate inefficiency attributable to air navigation services.** About 6% of unimpeded-trajectory fuel burn, with an ambition to reduce this to 2.3% by 2035. — [ICAO Stocktaking 2020, Perrin](https://www.icao.int:443/Meetings/Stocktaking2020/Documents/ICAO%20Stocktaking%202020%20-%202.3.6%20-%20Eric%20Perrin.pdf)
- **Older en-route benefit pool (2008 estimate).**
  - Horizontal en-route inefficiency: about 180 kg excess fuel per flight in Europe and 118 kg in the USA.
  - Total ANSP-actionable pool: 360 kg per flight in Europe and 330 kg in the USA.
  - Source: [Journal of Navigation, "4D trajectory ATM – killer apps part 2"](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E541C61DA7AD4961970BD26764C85C99/S0373463312000173a.pdf/4d-trajectory-air-traffic-management-are-there-killer-apps-part-2.pdf)
- **European ATM Master Plan 2025 targets.** Up to 200 Mt of CO2 saved by 2040 and 400 Mt by 2050. Operational efficiency could cut CO2 per flight by up to 6% by 2050. An EASA summary instead cites a goal of 9.3% (600 kg) less CO2 per average flight by 2050. The two figures may be different metrics. — [ASD Europe](https://www.asd-europe.org/news-media/news-events/news/european-air-traffic-management-masterplan-2025/); [EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/node/140864)
- **i4D trials (Airbus A320 MSN001, 2012 and 2014).** The target was 10% less environmental impact per flight. That is a goal, not a measured result. — [Aviation Today](https://www.aviationtoday.com/2014/03/20/airbus-conducts-sesar-i4d-flight-trials/)
- **ALBATROSS (Airbus, Air France, DSNA and others).** About 1,000 gate-to-gate demonstration flights. Results were not found. — [Aviation Today](https://www.aviationtoday.com/2021/10/01/albatross-demonstrate-4dt-can-reduce-co2-emissions-air-france-flights/)
- **Vendor claim (Viasat Iris datalink).** Continuous climb and descent save on average 500 kg of fuel per flight. This is a company claim. — [Viasat](https://viasat.com/enterprise-and-mobility/solutions/flight-operations-and-safety-solutions/iris)

**Contrail avoidance (a fuel penalty, not a saving)**
- **Google / American Airlines / Breakthrough Energy (2023).** 70 flights. Satellite imagery showed about 54% fewer contrails, for about 2% more fuel on the flights that deviated. Google estimated about 0.3% extra fuel fleet-wide, which is a projection. The cost was estimated at about US$5–25 per tCO2e. — [Energy Monitor](https://www.energymonitor.ai/newsletters/ai-cuts-climate-warming-contrails-of-planes-by-54-google-study); [AvWeb](https://avweb.com/?p=128987)
- **Larger randomised trial (results published March 2026).** About 2,400 US–Europe flights, January–May 2025, with Flightkeys flight planning.
  - The 112 flights that actually flew the avoidance plan produced 62% fewer contrails than the control group, with an estimated about 69% less contrail warming.
  - There was no statistically significant fuel difference between the two groups.
  - Across the 1,232 eligible flights, where dispatchers could opt in, contrails fell only about 12%.
  - Sources: [AP via WTOP, Mar 2026](https://wtop.com/europe/2026/03/american-airlines-and-google-say-ai-helped-airplanes-reduce-contrails-that-trap-heat/); [Hannah Ritchie](https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/contrails-google-ai)
- **EUROCONTROL MUAC / DLR live trial (January–October 2021).** 209 trajectories. Controllers applied vertical deviations of up to about 2,000 ft away from forecast ice-supersaturated regions (ISSRs), and statistical analysis indicated that contrails were avoided.
  - Problems found: precision of the meteorological tools, controller actions, and real-time feedback.
  - Later real-time simulation suggested capacity reductions of about 20% in moderate traffic, for safety reasons.
  - No fuel-penalty figure was published.
  - Sources: [EUROCONTROL press release](https://www.eurocontrol.int/press-release/mitigating-climate-impact-non-co2-emissions); [MUAC 2023 presentation](https://www.eurocontrol.int/sites/default/files/2023-11/2023-11-08-contrails-conference-session-009-sitova-ehrmanntraut-muac-contrails-prevention-project.pdf); [DLR](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2021/04/20211103_preventing-contrails-with-the-right-flight-altitude)
- **European Cockpit Association caveats.** ISSR prediction is not reliable, satellite verification is difficult, and the trade against extra CO2 needs more study. — [ATC Network](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/european-cockpit-association-publishes-paper-on-contrails)
- **Effect of 100% SAF on contrails (ECLIF3, A350, DLR/Airbus/Rolls-Royce/Neste).**
  - Measured: 56% fewer contrail ice crystals per kg of fuel burned compared with Jet A-1.
  - Modelled: 26% less contrail climate impact.
  - Earlier 50/50 blend (ECLIF2 with NASA): about half the ice crystals, and modelled 20–30% less contrail climate impact.
  - Sources: [DLR, June 2024](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2024/flying-using-100-percent-sustainable-aviation-fuel-significantly-reduces-non-carbon-dioxide-emissions); [Märkl et al., ACP 2024](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/3813/2024/acp-24-3813-2024-discussion.html); [Uni Mainz](https://press.uni-mainz.de/significantly-lower-climate-impact-of-contrails-when-using-sustainable-fuels/)

**Weight and systems that act in cruise**
- **787 no-bleed architecture.** Boeing is reported to claim about 3% better fuel burn from the no-bleed architecture, as part of the 787's overall advantage. Cabin pressurisation uses electrically driven compressors (about 75 kW each) instead of bleed air. The A350 is a hybrid that keeps bleed air, for example for wing anti-ice. The figure comes from a secondary reference site; no Boeing AERO article was found to confirm it. — [PlaneFYI](https://planefyi.com/systems/no-bleed-architecture/)
- **Cost of carrying weight.**
  - Rule of thumb: about 3.5% of the extra weight is burned per flight hour; one NBAA source gives a range of 2.5–5% per hour.
  - SAA: each kg saved on an international flight saves about 300 g of fuel.
  - Sources: [OpenAirlines](https://blog.openairlines.com/how-to-use-the-cost-of-weight-to-be-more-fuel-efficient); [Code7700/NBAA](https://code7700.com/takes_fuel_to_carry_fuel.htm); [Engineering News (SAA)](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/saa-seeks-to-cut-weight-on-its-long-range-flights-2015-02-24)
- **Fuel reserves (EASA fuel schemes, Decision 2022/005/R, effective 30 October 2022).**
  - The Basic Scheme keeps 5% contingency fuel.
  - The "Basic Scheme with Variations" allows 3% contingency when an en-route alternate is nominated, statistical contingency fuel, or Reduced Contingency Fuel.
  - These variations require a fuel-consumption monitoring system for each aircraft.
  - No quantified long-haul saving was found.
  - Source: [Honeywell](https://www.honeywellaerospace.com/us/en/about-us/news/2022/06/navigating-understanding-easa-latest-fuel-energy-requirements)
- **Aft-CG / trim-tank control (A330/A340).**
  - All A330s have a trim-tank transfer system that moves fuel aft in cruise to reduce trim drag. — [UBI thesis](https://ubibliorum.ubi.pt/bitstreams/1ed70aef-aa33-4dc2-99b2-5482b382daf6/download)
  - Airbus guidance says a further-aft CG within limits increases specific range, but gives no number. — [Airbus/ICAO 2006](https://www.icao.int:443/Meetings/EnvironmentalWorkshops/Documents/ICAO-TransportCanada-2006/Husse.pdf)
  - A weakly sourced reference site claims the A330 tail tank (about 6,200 kg) keeps CG near 38% MAC, cuts trim drag by up to 1.5% and saves 0.5–1.0% of total fuel. This is unverified. — [PlaneFYI](https://planefyi.com/pt/glossary/center-of-gravity/)

**Cost index (CI) and cruise Mach**
- **What CI is.** CI is the ratio of time-related cost to fuel cost. CI 0 gives maximum-range speed and minimum trip fuel.
  - Generic example: CI 0 gives about Mach 0.76, CI 100 about Mach 0.82, and CI 900 about Mach 0.84. The relationship is non-linear.
  - Narrowbody example from a secondary source: on a 1,000 nm sector, going from CI 0 to CI 100 saves about 9 minutes and costs about 400 kg of fuel. The first 20 CI points buy about a third of the time saving for about an eighth of the fuel penalty.
  - Sources: [Google Patents US8255148](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8255148); [Migflug](https://migflug.com/afterburner/?p=25752992)
- **CI is often set wrongly.** Airlines have been reported to tune CI to fit schedules, to approximate long-range cruise speed (LRC), or to copy values from other aircraft types. — [Edwards et al., Leeds](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91133/8/Edwards%20et%20al%20_)

### Inferences
- **Mission fuel baseline.** A 2040 300-seat widebody on a 6.5 h mission would probably burn on the order of 30–40 t of trip fuel. This is my own estimate from current A330neo/787 burn rates of roughly 5–7 t/h less a 2040 efficiency gain; it is not sourced. The per-flight figures below are rough:
  - **WER:** about 5% for the follower, about 2.5% averaged across a pair, and only for the formation share of cruise (about 80% in 2021). A 6.5 h medium-range sector over continental airspace is a weaker case than the North Atlantic, because pairing opportunities and oceanic procedures are concentrated on dense long-haul tracks. Realistic fleet-average benefit: about 0.5–1.5%.
  - **FRA/TBO:** 1–3% against today's European baseline. ToBeFREE's 27 kg per flight is small, being an increment over FRA that already exists. The ICAO 6% inefficiency pool sets the ceiling.
  - **Aft CG:** about 0.5–1.5%.
  - **3% contingency or statistical contingency fuel instead of 5%:** about 2% of trip fuel is carried less, i.e. roughly 0.6–0.8 t. At about 3.5%/h, carrying 0.7 t for about 6 h costs roughly 130–150 kg of fuel, about 0.4%.
  - **Each 1 t of empty weight removed:** about 200 kg of fuel per flight, about 0.5–0.7%.
  - **No-bleed architecture:** up to about 2–3% versus a bleed baseline, but this is a design choice and overlaps with engine-cycle gains.
  - **Contrail avoidance:** a cost of 0 to +2% on the affected flights, about 0.3% fleet-wide.
- **Additivity.**
  - WER, FRA/TBO, reserve policy and CI are largely additive with airframe and engine improvements, because they reduce drag-time or distance rather than specific fuel consumption (SFC).
  - Aft CG and weight savings interact with the wing and tail design. A design already optimised for relaxed stability gets less benefit from operational CG management.
  - WER and FRA/TBO partly conflict: formation pairs need fixed common tracks, whereas free routing gives each flight its own optimum.
  - Contrail avoidance can conflict with optimum cruise altitude, cruise climb and WER.
- **100% SAF and contrails.** SAF lowers soot and ice-crystal numbers (56% measured), so each contrail is less warming. Fewer flights would therefore justify avoidance deviations, which reduces the fuel penalty of contrail avoidance. Persistent contrails still form, though: the reported 26% modelled reduction in impact is far from 100%. Note that the Google 2025 trial found no significant fuel penalty in any case.
- **Cost index with SAF.** SAF prices are projected at about 2–3 times fossil Jet A-1 (not sourced here; check other researchers' notes). That raises fuel cost relative to time cost, which drives the optimal CI down towards LRC or maximum-range speed (MRC) and a slower cruise Mach. Fuel savings would plausibly be about 1–3% compared with today's typical CI. This reasoning comes from the CI definition and the non-linear CI–Mach curve; no SAF-specific study was found.
- **Cruise Mach as a design point.** If 2040 operators fly lower CI, the aircraft could be designed for a lower Mach (for example about 0.80–0.82 rather than 0.85), which compounds the airframe benefit. That makes cruise Mach a design requirement, not only an operating choice.

## Q2: Which require aircraft design features (flight-control software, structural margins for wake encounters, bleedless systems), and what are the certification and ATM requirements?

### Takeaway
Operational only, or mainly avionics/FMS:
- Free-route airspace (FRA), trajectory-based operations (TBO), cost index (CI), contrail avoidance and reserve policy. These need datalink, a 4D-capable FMS and fuel monitoring, which are standard on a 2040 type.

Design requirements:
- **Wake energy retrieval (WER)** needs flight-control and autopilot functions to hold position in the wake, a cockpit interface for the rendezvous, possibly structural and fatigue substantiation for continuous wake-vortex loading, and new ATM separation standards. Regulators have not yet certified any of this.
- **No-bleed systems and active aft-CG control** (trim tank, or fly-by-wire with relaxed stability) must be designed in from the start.

### Cited Findings
- **WER certification status.** The GEESE partners say the next step is to get authority support so that the operational concept can be certified. The 2025 trials kept full vertical separation within existing rules, i.e. no reduced-separation wake surfing was flown. — [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/?p=98378); [Airbus newsroom, Feb 2026](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026-02-geese-and-atm-wake-energy-retrieval-collaboration-achieves-new-milestones)
- **WER requires ATM involvement.** Rendezvous needs time-and-place guidance from ATC. In 2021 and 2025 multiple ANSPs took part: DSNA, NATS, NAV CANADA, IAA/AirNav Ireland and EUROCONTROL. — [AirInsight](https://airinsight.com/airbus-fellofly-validates-key-step-toward-5-fuel-cuts/); [French bee](https://prod.frenchbee.com/sites/default/files/2021-12/211109_FelloFlyFlight_VF_EN.pdf)
- **Wake prediction.** Boeing's ecoDemonstrator investigated wake prediction to allow extended-formation flight. — [Boeing](https://www.boeing.com/innovation/sustainability/ecodemonstrator/history)
- **Contrail avoidance and ATM.**
  - It needs ISSR forecasting and ATC tactical flight-level changes.
  - It can reduce airspace capacity by about 20% in moderate traffic.
  - It now works through flight-planning software (Flightkeys) without aircraft changes.
  - Sources: [EUROCONTROL](https://www.eurocontrol.int/news/eurocontrol-canso-sustainable-skies-conference-reducing-contrails-and-their-non-co2-effects); [WTOP/AP](https://wtop.com/europe/2026/03/american-airlines-and-google-say-ai-helped-airplanes-reduce-contrails-that-trap-heat/)
- **FRA savings need tools.** The ToBeFREE savings depend on an adequate FRA structure and ATC support tools. — [SESAR JU](https://sesarju.eu/node/689)
- **i4D/TBO.** Airbus flew i4D on an A320 with SESAR partners. It requires FMS 4D and datalink (ADS-C/CPDLC) capability. — [Aviation Today](https://www.aviationtoday.com/2014/03/20/airbus-conducts-sesar-i4d-flight-trials/)
- **Reserve variations.** The 3%+ERA and statistical contingency variations require a fuel-consumption monitoring system for each aircraft. — [Honeywell](https://www.honeywellaerospace.com/us/en/about-us/news/2022/06/navigating-understanding-easa-latest-fuel-energy-requirements)
- **No-bleed.** This is an architecture decision: electric cabin-air compressors and generators replace bleed. The A350 kept bleed for some functions. — [PlaneFYI](https://planefyi.com/systems/no-bleed-architecture/)
- **Trim tank / aft CG.** These are A330/A340 fuel-system design features that control CG automatically in cruise. — [UBI thesis](https://ubibliorum.ubi.pt/bitstreams/1ed70aef-aa33-4dc2-99b2-5482b382daf6/download); [SmartCockpit A340 fuel system](https://www.smartcockpit.com/wp_quiz/airbus-a340-fuel-system/)

### Inferences
- **Design-requirement list for a 2040 type:**
  - WER-ready autopilot and flight-control laws: wake-position hold, sideslip/roll trim and auto-rendezvous, plus a pilot interface.
  - Wake-loads fatigue substantiation of the airframe and tail.
  - A 4D FMS with datalink for TBO.
  - Electric no-bleed environmental control system (ECS) and ice protection.
  - Fuel-system or relaxed-stability fly-by-wire for cruise CG control.
  - A design cruise Mach chosen for SAF-era cost index.
  - Cabin weight minimisation.
  - It is cheaper to include WER functions at type design than to retrofit them, but they cannot be credited unless ATM separation rules change.
- **Purely operational:** CI selection, FRA/TBO use (given standard avionics), contrail-avoidance flight planning and reserve policy.
- **Realism for 2040:**
  - FRA and TBO: high. SESAR and NextGen deployment is expected to mature by 2035–2040.
  - Contrail avoidance: medium–high. It is software-based and needs no aircraft change.
  - WER: medium-low to medium for 2040. The concept works technically, but it needs ICAO or regional separation standards, ANSP pairing services and certification, and as of October 2026 none of these exist. Its benefit is also concentrated on oceanic long-haul rather than 6.5 h medium-range sectors.
  - No-bleed and aft CG: high. Both are proven technologies (787, A330).

### Gaps
- No public information found on the specific aircraft-side certification requirements for WER (flight-control laws, structural fatigue from wake loading, passenger-comfort criteria) from Airbus, EASA or the FAA.
- No ICAO separation-standard work item for paired flight was confirmed.
- No quantified data on the structural or fatigue penalty of sustained wake flight.
